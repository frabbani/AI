Phase 3 – Linear Algebra Mastery (Week 3–4)

Goal: Become fluent in high-performance matrix math.

----- Setup -----
A = random (5,5) matrix
B = random integer (5,5) matrix
v = random (5,) vector
X = random (8,3) matrix
y = random (8,) vector
M = random (6,6) matrix

1) Matrix operations
- Compute dot product of A and B using np.dot and @
- Compute matrix-vector product of A and v
- Compute L2 norm of v and angle (in radians) between v and another random vector w

2) Decompositions
- SVD of A: U, S, Vt = np.linalg.svd(A)
- Eigenvalues and eigenvectors of M

3) Solving Linear Systems
- Solve Ax = v for x (assume A is invertible)
- Compute inverse and pseudo-inverse of A

4) Orthogonalization
- QR decomposition of X
- Use QR to solve least-squares: X @ theta ≈ y

5) Mini-project: Linear Regression
- Implement Linear Regression using the Normal Equation:
  θ = (X.T @ X)^-1 @ X.T @ y
- Validate your solution vs. np.linalg.lstsq

Short Quiz (10)

1. What does np.dot(A, B) compute if A and B are both 2D arrays?
2. Which function computes the L2 norm of a vector?
3. What does np.linalg.svd return?
4. How do you solve Ax = b for x in NumPy?
5. What is the difference between np.linalg.inv and np.linalg.pinv?
6. What does QR decomposition provide?
7. In the normal equation for linear regression, what does (X.T @ X) need to be for the inverse to exist?
8. Which function returns both eigenvalues and eigenvectors?
9. What is the geometric meaning of the dot product between two vectors?
10. Why might you prefer the pseudo-inverse over the inverse in regression?

Answer Key

1. Matrix multiplication.
2. np.linalg.norm.
3. U, S, Vt (singular vectors and singular values).
4. np.linalg.solve(A, b).
5. inv is the true inverse (if exists), pinv is the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse (always exists).
6. Orthogonal (Q) and upper-triangular (R) matrices.
7. It must be invertible (full rank).
8. np.linalg.eig.
9. Measures similarity and projection; relates to the angle between vectors.
10. pinv works even if X.T @ X is not invertible (singular or ill-conditioned).
